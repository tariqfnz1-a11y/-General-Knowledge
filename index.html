The Big Picture & International Legal Standards

🌊 Open Sea & UNCLOS Framework
Governed internationally by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). Beyond 200 nautical miles (≈370 km) from baseline coasts lie the High Seas, constituting roughly 43% of Earth's surface (World Economic Forum), dedicated to peaceful uses and common heritage of humankind[cite: 1].
📜 Mare Liberum
Latin for “Free Sea”. Formulated by Dutch jurist Hugo Grotius in 1609, establishing that oceans are international territory open to all nations for trade and navigation[cite: 1].
🚫 Terra Nullius & Historical Critique
Latin for “land belonging to no one”. Historically weaponized during the colonial era to displace indigenous populations by falsely declaring inhabited lands legally vacant. In contemporary international law, true terra nullius is virtually non-existent except for unique anomalies.
🧊 Marie Byrd Land (Antarctica & The Antarctic Treaty System)
Covering over 1.6 million km², this is Earth's largest unclaimed landmass[cite: 1]. Under the 1959 Antarctic Treaty, territorial claims are frozen, dedicating the continent entirely to peaceful scientific cooperation[cite: 1].
🏜️ Bir Tawil (Superimposed Boundary Residual)
A 2,060 km² trapezoidal area along the Egypt–Sudan border created by conflicting colonial maps from 1899 and 1902. Because both nations claim the resource-rich Halaib Triangle[cite: 1], neither claims Bir Tawil, rendering it a modern land anomaly.
🏰 Gornja Siga & Micronationalism
A river-meander parcel along the Croatia–Serbia border left in legal limbo due to Danube river straightening. Claimed in 2015 as the "Free Republic of Liberland" by Vít Jedlička[cite: 1], though it fails standard criteria of statehood under the 1933 Montevideo Convention.

  • 12–200 nm (Exclusive Economic Zone / EEZ): Coastal states hold sovereign rights over natural resources (fishing, oil, minerals), while freedom of navigation is protected for all nations.
  • Beyond 200 nm (High Seas): International waters governed by the principle of common heritage under UNCLOS.
